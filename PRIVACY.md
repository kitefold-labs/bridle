# Privacy policy

*Bridle, by Kitefold Labs. Last updated: 1 November 2026.*

Bridle is a free, open-source SSH client for Android. This policy explains what happens to your
data when you use it. In short: **Bridle collects nothing, and Kitefold Labs runs no servers that
your data could go to.**

## What Bridle collects

Nothing. Bridle has:

- no accounts or sign-in;
- no analytics, telemetry, usage statistics or advertising identifiers;
- no ads and no third-party SDKs that track you;
- no crash-reporting service that sends data automatically;
- no servers. Kitefold Labs does not operate a backend for Bridle.

## Network connections

Bridle only connects to the SSH servers that **you** add, at the addresses you enter, and only
when you open a connection. It makes no other network requests. Links you tap (the source code,
licences, this policy, or a crash report you choose to file) open in your browser.

Port forwards that you create listen on your phone's loopback address (`127.0.0.1`) only, so other
devices on your network can't use them.

## Data stored on your phone

Everything you enter stays on your phone, in Bridle's private app storage:

- hosts, groups, Launch Profiles, snippets and port forwards;
- the host keys you have chosen to trust;
- your settings;
- SSH keys. Keys generated by Bridle are created inside the Android Keystore (secure hardware
  where available) and cannot be exported, by Bridle or anyone else. Keys you import are stored
  encrypted with a key held by the Android Keystore.

Passwords are never stored. Bridle excludes its data from Android's automatic cloud backup.
Uninstalling Bridle deletes all of it.

## Notifications

Bridle shows local notifications (for example, when Claude Code is waiting for you) only while a
connection to your server is open. There is no push service: nothing is sent to Google, Kitefold
Labs or anyone else to deliver them.

## Encrypted backups

You can export a backup of your configuration to a file. This happens only when you ask for it,
and the file goes where you choose (for example your phone's storage or a cloud drive you use).
The file is encrypted with a passphrase you choose. It never contains private keys or passwords.
Kitefold Labs never receives it.

## Crash reports

Bridle has no crash-reporting service, and nothing is ever sent automatically:

1. If Bridle crashes, it saves a report on your phone, with hostnames, usernames, IP addresses,
   file paths, key fingerprints and anything that looks like a secret removed, as well as the
   names of your saved hosts, Launch Profiles and tmux sessions. The report stays in Bridle's
   private storage.
2. On the next launch, Bridle asks whether you want to look at it. If you have turned on
   **Offer to report crashes** in Settings, it shows you the full text straight away instead.
   If you decline or discard it, the report is deleted.
3. If you choose to, Bridle opens a pre-filled issue on GitHub in your browser. You can edit or
   delete anything, and nothing is sent until you submit it yourself. GitHub's own privacy policy
   applies to issues you file there.

Terminal content is never part of a report. See
[docs/backup-and-privacy.md](docs/backup-and-privacy.md) for exactly what reports contain.

## Permissions

| Permission | Why |
|------------|-----|
| Internet, network state | To connect to your SSH servers and reconnect when the network changes |
| Notifications | To tell you about your sessions while connected |
| Foreground service | To keep your open sessions alive while Bridle is in the background |
| Biometrics | For key protection and the optional app lock, handled by Android; Bridle never sees your fingerprint or face data |

## Children

Bridle is a tool for system administration and is not directed at children.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published in the app's source repository and
noted in the release notes. Because Bridle collects no data, changes would only ever describe new
features that you choose to use.
